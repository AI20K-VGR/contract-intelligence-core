# Audience register

Load when the request names a reader other than the default (age 5). Each row says what
that reader already has, what they want out of the explanation, and which analogies land.
Missing the register is not fatal — missing the reader is.

One rule outranks every row below: **never talk down.** Simplifying is about removing
what the reader does not need, not about assuming they cannot follow.

## By age

| Reader | Vocabulary | Analogies that land | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Ordinary words only. One idea per sentence. | Toys, animals, food, the playground | Short. Concrete. "Imagine you have a box of crayons…" |
| 10 | Simple, but cause-and-effect is fine | School, sports, video games | A few steps, each following from the last |
| 15 | Some abstraction is fine | Phones, social apps, games | Slightly casual, never performatively so |
| 20–30 | Direct and clear | Work, money, daily life | Say the thing, then why it matters to them |
| 40+ | Respectful, assumes life experience | Owning a home, running a team, managing a household | Unhurried; no youth-culture shorthand |

## By education level

| Reader | Approach |
|---|---|
| 5th grade | No jargon at all. Concrete examples only. |
| Middle school | Terminology allowed if defined on first use. Step by step. |
| High school | Moderate complexity. Proper terms, explained. |
| College | Academic framing. Technical terms with brief context. Theory plus application. |
| Graduate | Assume the foundations. Spend the words on nuance, trade-offs, edge cases. |

## By role

| Reader | Cares about | Frame it as |
|---|---|---|
| Manager | Impact, timeline, risk, cost | Business outcome, team effect, the decision now on their desk |
| Engineer | Mechanism, architecture, trade-offs | How it works, what it costs, what it makes harder later |
| Designer | Experience, flow, visual result | What the user sees and does, interaction patterns, accessibility |
| Director | Strategy, return, position | Big picture, where resources go, what this wins or loses |
| Product manager | User value, priority, scope | Feature impact, what to build and what to skip |
| Colleague | Practical context | What changes for their work, what they need to collaborate |

## By relationship

| Reader | Tone | Analogies |
|---|---|---|
| Partner | Warm, patient, conversational | Shared routines, household tasks |
| Parent | Respectful, clear, never condescending | Technology they already use; bridge the generation, do not point at it |
| Child | Playful, encouraging, short | Games, cartoons, school, animals |
| Friend | Casual, some humour | Shared interests, pop culture, "you know how…" |

## Calibrating without a row

Two questions answer most readers the table does not list:

1. **What do they already have?** Anything they have is a foothold; anything they lack is
   a definition you owe them or a word you should not have used.
2. **What will they do with this?** An explanation that ends without touching what they
   do next is a lecture. Land on the "so what" for that specific reader.

## What never changes with the reader

- Evidence labels (`[OBSERVED]` / `[DERIVED]` / `[PRIOR]` / `[ASSUMED]`) and their
  sources. A five-year-old's explanation is still allowed to say a thing was measured.
- Evidence tokens: file paths, line numbers, identifiers, commit hashes, quotes. Simplify
  the sentence around them, never the token itself.
- Warnings, refusals, and stated uncertainty. Plainer wording, identical weight.
